# WriteWise: Complete Project Overview

> This document describes the WriteWise website in full: what it is, the problem it solves, who it's for, every
> page and tool, the design system, the technical architecture, SEO, privacy, monetization plans and the current
> status. It's written so that someone who has never seen the code can understand and review the project.

---

## 1. Summary

**WriteWise** (working brand name) is a free, privacy-first writing and study toolkit. It's a website with eight
browser-based text tools (word counter, character counter, case converter, text compare and more) plus original
guides about writing.

- **Target audience:** students, essay writers, job seekers, content creators and professionals in the
  **United States, Canada and the United Kingdom**.
- **Business model:** a free product that attracts organic Google search traffic through useful tools and original
  content, with possible **Google AdSense** monetization later.
- **Core promise:** every tool runs entirely in the visitor's browser. **Text is never uploaded to a server.**
- **Status:** feature-complete first version, production build passing, tested locally. **Not yet deployed** to a
  public URL.

---

## 2. The problem it solves

People constantly need small text utilities: checking an essay's word count before submitting, making a cover
letter fit a 1,500-character form field, fixing a heading's capitalization, or seeing what changed between two
drafts. Existing free tools in this space commonly have these problems:

1. **Privacy risk.** Many sites send pasted text to their servers. People paste unsubmitted essays, cover letters,
   contracts and confidential work documents with no idea where the text goes.
2. **Cluttered, hostile UX.** Pop-ups, autoplay video, ads between the input and the result, layout that jumps
   while typing, and email or sign-up walls.
3. **Unexplained numbers.** Two tools give different word counts for the same text and neither explains why, which
   matters when an assignment has a strict limit.
4. **Thin content.** Pages are often a tool plus keyword-stuffed filler text, with no useful guidance.

**WriteWise's answer:**

| Problem | WriteWise approach |
| --- | --- |
| Privacy risk | All processing in the browser with JavaScript. No text is transmitted. Users can verify this in the browser's Network tab, and the homepage tells them how. |
| Clutter | No pop-ups, no accounts, no ads (yet). Future ad positions are reserved away from the tools. |
| Unexplained numbers | Every tool page documents its exact counting rules. The homepage shows a "How we count" table generated by the real counting code. |
| Thin content | Each tool page has an original guide with instructions, worked examples and FAQs. Four in-depth guides answer real questions. |

## 4. The eight tools

All tools share these traits: live updates as you type, processing in the browser, a "Load an example" button,
Clear and Copy buttons where relevant, and correct handling of emoji, accented letters and CJK text. Every tool has
its own URL, H1, SEO title, meta description, explanatory guide, FAQ and related links.

### 4.1 Word Counter (`/tools/word-counter`)
- Live counts of words, characters, characters without spaces, sentences and paragraphs.
- Reading time (238 words per minute) and speaking time (140 wpm).
- Average words per sentence, plus a "most repeated words" list (common filler words excluded) to spot repetition.
- "Copy results" copies a formatted summary.

### 4.2 Character Counter (`/tools/character-counter`)
- Characters with and without spaces, words and lines.
- Custom character limit with presets (50, 100, 160, 280, 500, 1,000, 2,000).
- A progress bar that changes color at 90% and when over the limit, plus "X characters left" or "over by X".
- A toggle for whether spaces count toward the limit.

### 4.3 Reading Time Calculator (`/tools/reading-time-calculator`)
- Paste text, or enter a word count manually.
- Speed slider from 80 to 700 wpm, with presets: careful study (150), read aloud (183), average adult (238),
  fiction (260), fast reader (320), skimming (450).
- A comparison table of times at other speeds, and a reset button.

### 4.4 Essay Word Goal Tracker (`/tools/essay-word-goal-tracker`)
- Set a word target (presets from 250 to 5,000) and an allowed range (exact, ±5%, ±10%, ±15%).
- Shows completion percentage, words remaining, a progress bar and a status message ("Within the allowed range",
  "Over the upper limit by N").
- Estimated time to finish, based on a writing pace the user sets (words per hour, default 500). After two minutes of
  typing it switches to the user's live measured pace.
- Optional "Keep draft in this browser" (localStorage, off by default; turning it off deletes the saved draft).

### 4.5 Remove Duplicate Lines (`/tools/remove-duplicate-lines`)
- Removes repeated lines while keeping the first occurrence and the original order (nothing is sorted).
- Options: ignore case, ignore extra spaces (trim), remove empty lines.
- Shows original lines, final lines, duplicates removed and empty lines removed.
- "Copy output" and "Replace original with output". Handles 50,000 lines in about 150 ms.

### 4.6 Case Converter (`/tools/case-converter`)
- Five modes: Sentence case, Title Case, UPPERCASE, lowercase, Capitalized Case.
- Title Case follows the APA-style convention: articles, short conjunctions and prepositions of three letters or
  fewer stay lowercase, except as the first or last word or after a colon. Hyphenated words are capitalized in each
  part ("State-of-the-Art").
- Smart preservation: acronyms (NASA) and mixed-case words (iPhone) keep their capitals. Text typed with caps lock,
  or runs of shouted words ("THIS IS LOUD"), are converted normally.
- Sentence case fixes a lone "i" and its contractions ("i'm" → "I'm").

### 4.7 Paragraph Counter (`/tools/paragraph-counter`)
- Two modes: "Every line break" (like Microsoft Word) or "Blank lines only" (for hard-wrapped text from emails or
  PDFs).
- Paragraphs, words, characters, sentences, longest paragraph, average words and sentences per paragraph, and
  average sentence length.
- A paragraph-by-paragraph breakdown with relative length bars that flags paragraphs over 150 words.

### 4.8 Text Compare (`/tools/text-compare`)
- Two text panels (original and revised). Compares word by word or line by line.
- Uses the Myers diff algorithm. Adjacent changes are grouped ("many" → "dozens of" shows as one replacement).
- Insertions are underlined in indigo and deletions struck through in red, like proofreading marks.
- Statistics: words added, words removed, words unchanged and a similarity percentage.
- Swap, Reset and Ctrl/Cmd+Enter to compare. Warns when the result is out of date after edits.
- Falls back automatically to line-by-line comparison for very different long texts. 30,000 words compare in about
  140 ms.

### Counting rules (shared by all tools)
- **Words:** separated by spaces, line breaks, em dashes and en dashes. Must contain a letter or digit. Hyphenated
  words, contractions and numbers each count as one word. Chinese and Japanese characters count one word each.
- **Characters:** counted as user-perceived characters (grapheme clusters), so 👍🏽, "é" and family emoji each
  count as 1.
- **Sentences:** end with `. ! ? …` followed by a space or line break. Titles ("Dr."), initials ("J. K."),
  abbreviations followed by a lowercase word ("e.g. the") and decimals ("3.14") do not end a sentence. Line breaks
  also end sentences, so headings count.
- **Reading speed source:** Brysbaert (2019), *Journal of Memory and Language*, a meta-analysis of 190 studies:
  238 wpm for silent non-fiction, 260 for fiction and 183 for reading aloud.

## 7. Design system

**Concept: "the manuscript and the editor's blue pencil."** The site borrows from the world of writing and editing
rather than generic SaaS styling. The first version used purple gradients and icon cards and was rejected for looking
generic or AI-generated; it was redesigned around this concept.

| Element | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Background | Very light cool grey `#F5F6FA` (the desk); white `#FFFFFF` sheets |
| Text | Deep navy ink `#121A3D`; secondary `#2E3658`; muted `#5C6483` |
| Accent | Indigo `#4B3FD8`, the editor's blue pencil, used sparingly for active states, key numbers and insertions |
| Lines | Hairlines `#DCE0EC` |
| Headings | **Literata** (a book typeface) |
| Body | **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** (designed for legibility) |
| Manuscript and numbers | **Courier Prime** (standard manuscript format uses Courier) |
| Logo | A proofreader's insertion caret (‸) on a navy square, plus the word "WriteWise" |
| Signature element | Text inputs are typed manuscript sheets with the word count in the top-right corner, as in manuscript format. Statistics appear as margin notes. |
| Diff style | Proofreading marks: insertions underlined in indigo, deletions struck through in red |
| Dark mode | Full support: navy background, light ink, lighter indigo; follows system preference with a manual toggle and no flash on load |
| Deliberately avoided | Gradient text, gradient buttons, glowing blobs, icon-in-a-box cards, pill badges, generic "why choose us" grids, emoji in the UI |

Responsive from 360 px phones to desktop, with visible keyboard focus and reduced-motion support. Verified that no
page scrolls horizontally at 360 px.

## 8. Technical architecture

**Stack:** Next.js 16 (App Router), React 19, TypeScript 5, Tailwind CSS 4, lucide-react icons. No database, no paid
services, no external AI APIs.

**Rendering:** all 28 routes are prerendered as static HTML. Tool pages render their guide content on the server, so
search engines see the full text even though the tools are interactive client components. Each tool has its own
static route, so a page only loads its own tool's JavaScript.

```
src/
  app/            routes: homepage, tools, blog, legal pages, robots, sitemap, OG image, 404
  components/     header, footer, breadcrumbs, FAQ, search, JSON-LD, consent banner, ad slot
  tools/          interactive tool UIs (client components) + shared UI kit
  content/        guide text and FAQs for each tool page and blog article
  lib/text/       pure text engines: stats, case, dedupe, diff (no dependencies, unit-tested)
  lib/            tool and post registries, SEO helpers, site config
  lib/ai/         provider-neutral interface for future AI features (disabled)
  lib/plans.ts    free/pro entitlement layer (everything is free today)
tests/            unit tests (Node test runner)
scripts/          link and metadata crawler
docs/             EXTENDING.md (AI and paid APIs), ADSENSE-READINESS.md
```

**Performance (measured locally on the production build):**
- About 145 KB of gzipped JavaScript per page, most of it the React and Next.js runtime. The site's own code adds a
  few KB.
- About 10 KB of CSS. Fonts are self-hosted at build time (no requests to Google Fonts at runtime).
- The text-input hot path uses React `useDeferredValue` so typing stays responsive on large documents.
- Text counting handles 160,000 words in well under a second.
- Targets: LCP < 2.5 s, INP < 200 ms, CLS < 0.1. Not yet measured with PageSpeed Insights because the site isn't
  deployed.

**Deployment options (free):**
- **Vercel**, with standard Next.js output and security headers from `next.config.ts`.
- **Cloudflare Pages or Netlify**, via a fully static export (`npm run build:static` → `out/` folder), with a
  `public/_headers` file.

**Environment variables (all optional):** `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_CONTACT_EMAIL`,
`NEXT_PUBLIC_GSC_VERIFICATION`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_GA_ID` and `AI_PROVIDER` (reserved).

## 9. SEO implementation

- Unique title, meta description and a single H1 on every page. Logical H2/H3 structure.
- Canonical URLs, Open Graph and Twitter card metadata, and a generated 1200×630 share image.
- XML sitemap (20 URLs) and robots.txt pointing to it. No accidental noindex (only the 404 page is noindex).
- Breadcrumbs with BreadcrumbList structured data. WebSite and Organization on the homepage, WebApplication on tool
  pages, BlogPosting on articles. FAQPage markup was deliberately left out, because Google now only shows FAQ rich
  results for government and health sites.
- Clean URLs, crawlable internal links (related tools, related guides, footer and contents lists) and correct status
  codes.
- Content written in natural American English, with British and Canadian conventions explained where relevant.
- **No invented data:** no claims about search volume, keyword difficulty, traffic or rankings.
- An automated crawler (`npm run check-links`) verifies links, status codes, titles, descriptions, canonicals, H1
  count, Open Graph tags, sitemap coverage and 404 behavior. It currently passes.

## 10. Privacy and trust

- Tool text is processed only in the browser. No tool sends text anywhere.
- With no configuration, **no cookies and no third-party scripts** are used.
- localStorage is used only for: the theme choice, the optional Essay Goal Tracker draft (opt-in), and the analytics
  consent choice (only if analytics is configured).
- **Analytics (optional):** if a GA4 ID is set, a consent banner appears. Google Analytics loads only after the
  visitor clicks "Allow analytics", and visitors can change their choice via "Cookie settings" in the footer. Tested:
  no GA script or cookies before consent.
- The Privacy Policy is generated from the actual configuration: it only mentions Google Analytics when GA is
  enabled. It covers UK GDPR, EU GDPR, PIPEDA (Canada) and US state laws such as the CCPA.
- Pages: About (principles and editorial standards), Contact (email, no form), Privacy Policy, Terms of Use and
  Disclaimer (results are estimates; no affiliation with Microsoft, Google, Common App or UCAS).
- Each tool page shows a clear line: "Runs in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded."

## 11. Monetization readiness (AdSense)

- **No ads are implemented** and no placeholder ads are shown.
- An `AdSlot` component marks reserved positions (below the tool, inside long content, end of articles) and
  currently renders nothing. Ads will never sit inside a tool or between its input and results.
- `docs/ADSENSE-READINESS.md` is a checklist: content quality, trust pages, Search Console, `ads.txt`, a
  **Google-certified consent management platform** (required for UK/EEA ad serving; the simple analytics banner
  doesn't qualify), CLS-safe fixed ad heights, and Privacy Policy updates.

## 12. Built-in extensibility for AI or paid features

The owner wants to be able to add AI or paid-API features later without weakening the privacy promise:

- Each tool declares `processing: "local" | "server"`. Only local tools show the "processed in your browser" badge;
  a server tool automatically shows a warning instead.
- Each tool declares `tier: "free" | "pro"`, gated through `src/lib/plans.ts`.
- `src/lib/ai/` defines a provider-neutral interface (`AIProvider`, `AIRequest`, `AIResponse`), selected by the
  `AI_PROVIDER` environment variable and disabled by default. Adding a provider means writing one adapter file.
- `docs/EXTENDING.md` gives step-by-step instructions, including the server route, API key handling, rate
  limiting and the required privacy policy updates.

## 14. Known limitations and open items

- **Not deployed yet.** Needs a public URL, then Google Search Console verification and sitemap submission.
- `NEXT_PUBLIC_CONTACT_EMAIL` must be set before launch; until then the Contact page shows a "not configured" notice.
- Legal pages are a careful starting point but **should be reviewed by the owner** (they aren't legal advice).
- The Vercel free (Hobby) plan restricts commercial use, so check the terms before adding ads, or use Cloudflare Pages.
- Only four guides so far. Organic traffic depends on adding more genuinely useful content over time; no ranking is
  guaranteed.
- Real Core Web Vitals scores can only be measured after deployment.
- Brand name "WriteWise" is a working name; trademark and domain availability haven't been checked.
- No offline/PWA caching beyond the browser's normal behavior.
